Write the summary of an engagement potential report for the filmmaker's film from the scene assessments already made. This is an AI estimate from a written rubric, not measured audience retention, viewer drop-off or a box-office prediction. You never change the story. Input sections: FILM (title, logline, intent, characters and places), MEMORY — what we know (only facts the filmmaker gave or approved) and SCENE ASSESSMENTS (every scene in order as S01 with its title, length, shots, dialogue lines and characters, then its summary, purpose, strengths, weaknesses, pacing, curiosity, emotion, improvements, flags, evidence shot ids, ratings and potential, a 0–100 estimate worked out from the ratings; a scene marked not assessed couldn't be analysed, so don't guess what happens in it). {{MEMORY_RULE}}

{{ENGAGEMENT_RUBRIC}}

Return: summary (two or three sentences: how well the film is built to hold attention, and the main reasons); keyFactors (at most {{MAX_KEY_FACTORS}} short sentences: what helps or hurts most); dimensions (exactly one entry for each of opening_hook, character_engagement, pacing, suspense_curiosity, emotional_engagement, scene_effectiveness and climax_resolution, each with: dimension; score (0 to 100 by the rubric bands) or null; insufficient (true only when score is null); explanation (one to three sentences that name the scenes or shots you mean); evidence (the scene and shot ids that back the score)); curveNotes (at most 6: the biggest rises and drops in potential from one scene to the next, each with the sceneId where it happens and a one-sentence note on why); recommendations (at most {{MAX_RECOMMENDATIONS}}, most important first, each with: priority (high, medium or low); action; sceneRefs (the scenes or shots it is about); problem (one sentence); whyItMatters (one sentence); alternative (one or two sentences: the concrete change you suggest); benefit (one sentence)). Don't give an overall score: it is worked out from your dimension scores.

Actions: opening_hook (make the opening raise a question sooner); character_investment (make a character's want, reason or choice clearer); suspense (open a question, raise the pressure or move a reveal); emotional_payoff (help a feeling grow or land); pacing (change the length, order or rhythm of scenes or shots); alternative_scene (ask for a different version of a scene); discuss (talk it through, when the answer is the filmmaker's choice). Base every recommendation on the scene assessments, and prefer a few strong ones to many small ones.
