# Engagement rubric

Engagement potential is how well the story, as written, is built to hold attention. Judge it only from what the story shows: its scenes, shots and dialogue, and the facts the filmmaker gave or approved. It is an estimate from this rubric, not a measurement and not a prediction about real viewers.

Scene ratings. tension: 1 = nothing is at risk; 2 = a small worry; 3 = a clear problem or conflict; 4 = the pressure rises and something could be lost; 5 = a peak where what a character cares about most is at risk. curiosity: 1 = no open question; 2 = a small question; 3 = a clear question that pulls toward the next scene; 4 = a strong mystery, or a reveal that changes what we know; 5 = the question the whole film turns on is raised or answered. emotion: 1 = flat; 2 = a light feeling; 3 = a clear feeling we share with a character; 4 = a strong feeling tied to what a character wants or fears; 5 = a turning point in a relationship, or a deep loss or win. change: 0 = nothing is different by the end of the scene; 1 = something shifts (new information, a small decision, a change of mood); 2 = the story turns (a big decision, a reveal, or a win or loss that changes what happens next). A quiet scene with low tension can still rate high on emotion or change.

Dimension scores run from 0 to 100 in five bands: 85 or more = strong; 70–84 = solid, with small gaps; 50–69 = mixed, working in places with clear problems in others; 30–49 = weak, the problems outweigh what works; under 30 = very weak or barely there.

- opening_hook (the first scenes, about the first minutes: something to wonder about, a clear premise, how fast the central situation appears): 85+ = the first shots raise a strong question and the central situation is clear within the first scene; 70–84 = a clear question and premise that arrive a little slowly; 50–69 = the premise comes through, but late, or the opening question is weak; 30–49 = a long setup with little to wonder about; under 30 = no clear premise or question in the opening.
- character_engagement (what the main characters want, why they want it, what they could lose, and the choices they make): 85+ = a clear want and reason, something real to lose, and choices that drive the story; 70–84 = clear wants and choices with a few thin spots; 50–69 = we know what they want, but they mostly react instead of choosing; 30–49 = wants and reasons are vague and choices are few; under 30 = no clear want or choice.
- pacing (the story keeps moving: little repetition or explaining, no long stretch where nothing develops, a good mix of action, dialogue, feeling and quiet): 85+ = every stretch develops something and the rhythm changes on purpose; 70–84 = moves well with one slow or rushed spot; 50–69 = some repetition, too much explaining, or a stretch where nothing develops; 30–49 = several slow or repeated stretches, or the rhythm hardly changes; under 30 = the story stands still for long stretches.
- suspense_curiosity (open questions, rising pressure, reveals, and scene endings that pull forward without fake cliffhangers): 85+ = questions open and close at a steady pace, the pressure builds and reveals land; 70–84 = clear questions and some build, with one flat stretch; 50–69 = a few questions but little build, or reveals come too early; 30–49 = little to wonder about and the pressure rarely rises; under 30 = no open questions.
- emotional_engagement (feelings that grow, relationships, turning points, and what the characters could lose): 85+ = feelings grow toward clear turning points tied to a relationship or a loss; 70–84 = clear feelings with one turning point that could land harder; 50–69 = feelings are named more than shown, or stay at one level; 30–49 = feelings are thin or unconnected to what happens; under 30 = flat throughout.
- scene_effectiveness (each scene changes something: a decision, new information, a conflict or a feeling): 85+ = nearly every scene changes something; 70–84 = most scenes do, one or two only add mood; 50–69 = several scenes change little; 30–49 = many scenes repeat or stand still; under 30 = scenes rarely change anything.
- climax_resolution (setups pay off, the ending is earned, the central conflict is resolved): 85+ = the main setups pay off and the ending follows from the characters' choices; 70–84 = a satisfying ending with a loose thread; 50–69 = the ending works but feels rushed, lucky or partly unearned; 30–49 = key setups are dropped or the ending ignores the central conflict; under 30 = no real resolution. When the story has no ending yet, give score null with insufficient true.

Rules. Every rating and score must rest on what you cite: shot ids such as S02_SH03 or scene ids such as S02, taken from the input. When the story doesn't show enough to judge something, say so: give score null with insufficient true instead of guessing, and never fill a gap with what stories like this usually do. Never recommend removing or cutting a scene just because it is quiet, slow or low in tension: a quiet scene can carry feeling, give the film room to breathe and change the pace; suggest how it could do that better instead. Suggest removing a scene only when it repeats another scene, changes nothing or only explains. Never say what real viewers or audiences will do, and never give percentages, drop-off, watch time, retention or box-office predictions.
